# Lalabase Trademark Policy

Lalabase is open source software. The [AGPLv3](LICENSE) gives you broad rights
to the code: you may use it, study it, change it, share it and run it as a
service. The license does not give you rights to the **name** Lalabase or to
its **logo**. This policy explains how you may use them.

The goal is simple: when people see the name Lalabase, they should know what
they get and who stands behind it.

## The marks

This policy covers:

- the name **Lalabase**,
- the Lalabase logo and favicon.

Lalabase is a registered trademark of gboyke solutions UG (haftungsbeschränkt),
Germany ("we" in this policy), at the German Patent and Trade Mark Office (DPMA).

## The short version

- **You may** talk about Lalabase, run it, build on it and say that your work
  is based on it.
- **You may not** call your own version, product or service "Lalabase", or make
  it look like the official project or an official service.

## How this relates to the AGPL

- The AGPL is a copyright license. Trademarks are a separate right. Section 7(e)
  of the AGPLv3 expressly allows a project to decline trademark rights.
- This policy does not take away anything the AGPL grants for the code. You can
  still copy, change, run, share and host Lalabase. For a changed version or a
  service you offer to others, you just use your own name.
- Even where a file that shows a mark is distributed under the AGPL, the
  copyright license does not grant rights to use that mark.
- Keeping copyright notices, license notices and the project history intact is
  required by the AGPL. Doing so is not a trademark use and needs no permission.

## Uses that need no permission

1. **Talking about Lalabase.** Articles, reviews, comparisons, talks, tutorials,
   courses, videos and social media posts may name Lalabase, as long as what
   you say is accurate.
2. **Using Lalabase.** You can run Lalabase for yourself or your organisation,
   unmodified or with your own configuration, and call it Lalabase inside your
   organisation.
3. **Describing a relationship honestly.** You may write "based on Lalabase",
   "a fork of Lalabase", "works with Lalabase" or "integration for Lalabase",
   if your own name is more prominent than ours and you do not suggest an
   official connection.
4. **Offering services around Lalabase.** You may say, in plain words, that you
   install, host, support or teach Lalabase, for example "We host and support
   Lalabase for your team". Your service carries your own name and brand, and
   you do not use the Lalabase logo.
5. **Sharing unmodified copies.** You may distribute Lalabase under its name,
   for example as a Linux package or container image, if the software is
   unmodified apart from packaging changes and security fixes published by the
   project, and you do not present your build as the official one.
6. **Linking to the project.** You may use the unmodified logo to link to the
   official Lalabase website or repository, or to illustrate an article about
   Lalabase.
7. **Community activities.** Non-commercial user groups, meetups and events may
   use the name, for example "Lalabase User Group Hamburg", if it is clear that
   we do not run them.

## Uses that need written permission

1. Using "Lalabase", or a name that is easy to confuse with it, as the name of a
   modified version, a fork or another product. Examples: "LalaBase",
   "Lala Base", "Lalabase Plus", "OpenLalabase".
2. Using the name in the name of a hosted service, a company, a domain, an app
   store listing, a package or a social media account in a way that suggests it
   is official. Examples: "Lalabase Cloud" for your own hosting,
   "lalabase-hosting.example", "@lalabase_support".
3. Suggesting that we endorse, certify, partner with or sponsor you or your
   product.
4. Changing the logo, combining it with your own marks, or using it as the
   brand of your own product, service or marketing.
5. Selling merchandise with the name or the logo.

## Modified versions and forks

If you distribute a modified version of Lalabase, or offer a modified version
as a service to other people:

- **Choose your own name and logo.** Replace the Lalabase name and logo
  wherever your users see them: the product name in the interface, page titles,
  emails, the login and registration pages, the web app manifest and any public
  or marketing pages.
- **These may stay as they are:** identifiers in the code (such as the
  `Lalabase::` module, class names, file paths, database names and
  configuration keys), copyright and license notices, the commit history, and an
  honest statement that your software is based on Lalabase.
- **What counts as modified:** anything beyond changing settings the software
  supports (for example environment variables or admin settings), packaging
  changes, and security fixes published by the project.

## Writing the name

- Write **Lalabase** as one word with a capital L. Not "LalaBase", "Lala Base"
  or "lalabase" (except in code, commands and URLs).
- Use it as a name, not as a verb or a generic term for project software.

## Questions, permission and misuse

Write to **info@lalabase.com** in German or English. For a permission request,
tell us what you plan to do and where the name or logo would appear. If you see
a use that confuses people, please let us know.

When a use does not fit this policy, we will contact the people behind it first
and look for a friendly solution.

## Changes and legal scope

We may update this policy. The version in this repository applies. This policy
does not restrict uses that the law allows without permission, such as honest
descriptive references. It is not legal advice.
